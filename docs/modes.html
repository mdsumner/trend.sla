What this is

Each cell's monthly mean comes straight from the published sums (summed sumwx over summed sumw for the cell and month), so nothing here is re-read from the source. From each cell's monthly series the mean, a linear trend and the mean seasonal cycle are removed. The singular value decomposition of the resulting month by cell matrix gives the modes, with cells equally weighted (S2 level-5 cells are roughly equal in area).

A mode is a fixed spatial pattern, shown on the map, multiplied by its time series below. The map is in millimetres per standard deviation of the time series, so pattern times series is that mode's contribution to sea level in mm. Mode 1 is signed so that it is positive where the Nino 3.4 box (5S-5N, 170W-120W) loads.

The pattern and time series together are a compression of the record: each cell gets a vector of loadings, each month a vector of amplitudes. That is the classical form of what are now called embeddings, and the baseline any learned embedding of this record should be measured against.

Masked: the Caspian Sea

The CMEMS grid includes the Caspian, which is a lake, not ocean. The first unmasked analysis gave it a mode of its own (12% of variance): a fall of about 1.5 m from 1995 to 2021, then a rise of about 1.2 m in little more than a year from September 2022. The fall is in line with the long decline of the Caspian. The abrupt rise is more likely a change in how the product treats the lake than a real signal; that is an open question to check. The Caspian's 14 cells are excluded here. They are still part of the record's global series.
